Research

Why lead free perovskites

The best perovskite solar cells still contain lead, and that is a big reason they are not on rooftops yet. Tin based perovskites are safer, but they still trail on efficiency and stability. My work is about closing that gap.

Tin based perovskite solar cells

Films and devices processed in an inert atmosphere glovebox by spin coating and thermal evaporation, then characterized with FESEM, XRD and UV Vis spectroscopy.

FabricationCharacterizationDevice modeling

Tunnel FET device physics

Sentaurus TCAD studies of step tunneling path and double gate TFETs: heavy ion radiation, interface trap charges and temperature. Three peer reviewed papers.

Model the device

Simulate interfaces and traps to explain the result and decide what to change next.

Solar cells without lead have to work as well as the ones with it. I build them, measure them, and *model* *what* *happens* *inside.*

FAQ

Common questions

What do you research?

Lead free, tin based perovskite solar cells, across fabrication, characterization and device simulation. I'm a PhD researcher at the School of Energy Science and Engineering, IIT Guwahati, supervised by Dr. Ajay Singh.

Why work on tin instead of lead?

The best perovskite cells still contain lead, which is a big reason they are not on rooftops yet. Tin based perovskites are safer but still trail on efficiency and stability. My work is about closing that gap.

Are you open to research collaborations?

Yes, now. The fastest way to start is an email to j.saha@iitg.ac.in.

Are you looking for postdoc or R&D roles?

After my PhD, yes. I'm interested in postdoctoral and R&D roles in photovoltaics.

How did you move from transistors to solar cells?

My MTech at NIT Silchar was on tunnel FET device physics in Sentaurus TCAD, which produced three peer reviewed papers. That background is why I treat a solar cell as a device first: interfaces, trap states and band alignment.
